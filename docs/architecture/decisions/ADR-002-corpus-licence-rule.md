# ADR-002: Corpus licence rule — ingest only documents verified as eligible

- **Status:** Decided for the Sprint 2 test corpus (team decision recorded from the project handoff). Not yet ratified by the client, counsel or the publishers; held documents stay held until someone with that authority approves them.
- **Decision:**
  - Only documents with `eligible=yes` in [`data/sources/corpus_manifest.csv`](../../../data/sources/corpus_manifest.csv) may be ingested. Anything else (`eligible=no`, blank, misspelt or missing) is skipped. The manifest decides, not the contents of the download folder: a PDF sitting in `data/corpus/originals/` is not eligible just because it is there.
  - "Eligible" means the reuse terms were read from a primary source (the legal notice inside the PDF, or the publisher's own policy page), not from a blog, a summary or memory. Each manifest row records what was read, on what date, and what is still unverified.
  - Held for now: ICH Q1A(R2), Q3B(R2), Q6A, Q8(R2) (the PDFs state no reuse terms); WHO TRS 1010 Annex 10 (non-commercial licence); CDSCO BA/BE guidelines 2005 (permission required, no official copy found); EMA nitrosamine Q&A (terms not checked); the Indian Pharmacopoeia (licensed content, client's licence unknown).
  - Every citation shows the manifest's `attribution` text for its document, next to the passage, document version and page. If the platform shows adapted text such as a summary, it labels it as adapted. Nothing implies that a regulator endorses the platform.
  - Until a scientist has reviewed the golden questions, any demo output carries the label "not yet reviewed by a domain expert".
- **Why:** Nobody on the team can judge copyright or licensing, and a wrong call is hard to undo once text is indexed and shown to users. "Free to read" is not the same as "free to copy, index and show": a licence can allow reading but forbid commercial reuse. A conservative rule avoids the judgement entirely. It also implements PRD risk R-04 (source licences may not permit the intended use) and requirements FR-04 (record the authorization basis of every item) and FR-08 (index only eligible passages).
- **Trade-offs:**
  - The corpus is small: 15 documents, only two of them Indian, and no scientific articles with a verified plain CC BY licence. This is a limit of what is clearly reusable today, not a judgement of what matters to Indian formulation work.
  - Several widely used ICH guidelines (Q1A, Q8, Q6A, Q3B) are held, so some real questions will correctly be answered "not covered by approved sources" (see golden questions GQ-15 and GQ-16).
  - The licence readings are lay readings, not legal advice. Open points are recorded in the manifest: the ICH notice was read through a fetch tool and page 2 of one PDF still needs a human check; the FDA policy page does not name guidance PDFs; the Gazette reading (Copyright Act 1957, s.52(1)(q)(i)) applies to the Gazette copy only.
  - The rule is checked per document, so content that a document quotes from a third party is not covered by the ICH notice.
- **Revisit when:** someone with authority approves a held document; ICH adopts the consolidated Q1 (expected around Nov 2026, and it may carry the current notice); the client supplies an approved source list or licences (Indian Pharmacopoeia, USP-NF); EMA's terms are checked; ICH, FDA or EMA publish a revision of a core document; the manifest is re-checked before any real ingestion.
